How to start a conversation about faith

You don’t need a perfect opening line. Begin naturally, listen well, and ask permission before taking a conversation deeper.

Read the guide

1. Begin with the person, not a script. Be present in the situation you share. A friendly greeting or a question about what is already happening can be a more natural start than launching into a prepared speech.

2. Ask an open question. If the moment feels appropriate, try something simple such as, “Is faith something you ever think about?” or “Would you be comfortable talking about spiritual things?” Accept a no graciously.

3. Listen before responding. Give the person room to finish. Ask what they mean or what shaped their view. Listening helps you respond to the person in front of you rather than assuming what they believe.

4. Share honestly and briefly. Explain what you believe and why it matters to you. Make space for questions, and don’t pressure someone to agree or make a decision on the spot.

Keep the conversation respectful and pay attention to the other person’s comfort. A kind ending is better than pushing past a boundary.

How to explain the Gospel simply

A short outline can help you explain the Christian message clearly without turning a conversation into a lecture.

Read the guide

God: God made us and invites us to know him. He is good, just, and the source of life.

Us: All of us have sinned and turned away from God. We cannot undo that or earn our way back by trying harder.

Jesus: Jesus lived without sin, died for sinners, and rose again. Christians believe that through him God offers forgiveness and new life.

Our response: The invitation is to turn to Jesus and trust him. Faith is not a way to earn God’s love; it is relying on what Jesus has done.

Use your own words, pause to ask what the person thinks, and invite questions. You can also read a Gospel account together instead of trying to cover everything in one conversation.

What to do when you don’t know the answer

You can be honest about a hard question without dismissing it or pretending to know more than you do.

Read the guide

First, listen carefully and make sure you understand the question. You could say, “That’s a thoughtful question—what part of it matters most to you?” Avoid guessing what someone is really asking.

It is okay to say, “I don’t know, but I’d like to look into that.” Don’t make up an answer or rush to change the subject. If the person is open to it, agree to return to the question after you have learned more.

Look for a trustworthy source, such as the Bible itself or a knowledgeable Christian you trust. Then follow through. If you still don’t know, be honest about that too.

A conversation is not a debate you have to win. Treating someone’s question with patience and respect is part of sharing your faith well.

How to follow up without pressure

A thoughtful follow-up can show care while leaving the other person free to continue—or not continue—the conversation.

Read the guide

If the person welcomes another conversation, agree on a simple next step together: share a resource, read a passage of Scripture, or meet again to talk about a question they raised.

Keep any promise you made. If you said you would find an answer, get back to them. If you are sharing a link or book, briefly explain why you thought it might help.

Ask before contacting them again, respect their preferred way to communicate, and accept it if they aren’t interested. Do not treat someone’s contact details, story, or spiritual questions as something to share with others without their permission.

You can pray for someone privately without making them feel that they owe you a response. The goal is to care for a person, not to secure a result.
